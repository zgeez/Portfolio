Electromagnetic Kinetic 7-Segment Display

A single-digit, ultra-low-power indicator that swaps LEDs for steel-core coils and magnets: built to showcase precision electromechanics, energy-aware firmware, and rapid-prototyped mechanics.

Why a Moving-Segment Digit?

Traditional LED displays idle at ≈250 mA; my goal was a tactile, daylight-readable indicator that sips <3 mA average. Each segment is a PLA bar with an N35 magnet; a 400-turn screw-core coil repels or attracts it for 25 ms, then consumes nothing until the next change.

Architecture

  • Mechanics: M3 screw acts as low-cost steel core; PLA rail ensures sub-0.1 mm skew, so a 1 A pulse yields 7 mm travel.
  • Electronics: Seven TB6612 half-bridges tie directly to 14 GPIOs; future rev will multiplex through a 74HC595 to free I/O.
  • Firmware: Segment font in flash; loop XORs desired vs current mask and energises only changed bars (≈3 pulses per minute).
  • Energy: Pulse: 0.75 A · 25 ms = 0.018 mAh; 8 pulses/min → 2.5 mA avg vs 250 mA LED block → 100 × savings.

What Worked & What's Next

  • Successes: repeatable motion, no hold current, and hot-swap coils proved the mechanical concept.
  • Pain points: initial coils overheated until I added XOR gating; rail printing demanded ±0.02 mm tolerance.
  • Roadmap: shift-register I/O, air-core/keeper redesign to halve pulse current, BLE OTA updates, and a 4-digit backplane PCB.

Built with Fusion 360, KiCad, STM32CubeIDE, and far too many coffee beans.
